# SLIDE 9: BUSINESS MODEL
## Visual: Simple flow diagram

**ON SCREEN:**

> **How We Make Money**
>
> ```
> User searches "running shoes"
>        ↓
> Fiber finds best deal at Finish Line ($170, 3.25% cashback)
>        ↓
> User buys through Fiber's affiliate link
>        ↓
> Finish Line pays Fiber 6.5% commission = $11.05
>        ↓
> Fiber keeps 3.25% ($5.52) ← OUR REVENUE
> User gets 3.25% ($5.53) ← THEIR CASHBACK
> ```
>
> **Unit Economics**
> | Metric | Value |
> |--------|-------|
> | Avg order value | $150 |
> | Purchases/user/month | 2 |
> | Take rate | ~3% |
> | Revenue/active user/month | $9 |
> | CAC (organic/protocol) | $2–5 |
> | LTV (12 months) | $80–110 |
> | LTV:CAC | 20:1+ |

**YOU SAY (1 min):**
"The business model is straightforward. Merchants pay us affiliate commissions — typically 3-8% — when a user buys through Fiber. We split that with the user as cashback. On a $150 purchase, we might earn $11 and give back $5.50 to the user. Everyone wins: the merchant gets a customer, the user gets cashback, we get revenue.

Unit economics are strong. Average order value of $150, about two purchases per active user per month, and a 3% net take rate gives us $9 per active user per month. Customer acquisition cost is low — near zero for agent-protocol users, $2-5 for direct users. That gives us an LTV-to-CAC ratio above 20:1. These are Shopify-tier economics."

# Q&A PREPARATION

## Tier 1: Questions They WILL Ask

**Q: "Who's on the team?"**
A: [Fill in — be honest about strengths. If solo, say: "I'm the technical founder. I've built the MVP solo in [X weeks]. I'm looking for a co-founder with commerce/growth experience. Seed funds will help attract that person."]

**Q: "What's your revenue today?"**
A: "We're pre-revenue. The product is live but we haven't monetized yet. This round funds the go-to-market. Our unit economics model shows $9/active user/month at scale."

**Q: "How do you acquire users?"**
A: "Three channels. One: agent platforms — when agents need to shop, they call our API. Zero CAC. Two: organic — SEO, Product Hunt, AI communities. Three: referral cashback — users invite friends for bonus rewards. We expect 60%+ of growth to come from the protocol layer, which is effectively free distribution."

**Q: "What if Google/Amazon builds this?"**
A: "Amazon can't build this — they'd be disintermediating themselves. Their business is selling their inventory at their margin. Google could, but they're slow in commerce. More importantly, agents won't use a Google-only solution. They'll use the open protocol that gives them the best deals across all merchants. That's us. Same reason the web won over AOL."

**Q: "What if agents don't take off for shopping?"**
A: "Two things. First, even our conservative model — agents handling just 5% of online shopping — represents a $265 billion market. Second, Fiber works for humans too. The chat interface, the comparison, the cashback — all of that works without agents. Agents are our thesis, but we're not dependent on them for early traction."

**Q: "Why crypto? Isn't that risky?"**
A: "Crypto is optional, not core. 80% of users will use simple cloud cashback — no wallet, no tokens, just credit on Fiber. The crypto option exists for early adopters who want self-custody of their rewards. It's a user acquisition channel for the Web3 community, not a business dependency."

**Q: "What's your moat?"**
A: "Three things compound over time. One: the merchant network — 50,000+ merchants integrated, each one is a relationship that takes time to replicate. Two: user data — the Soul Engine learns preferences that make recommendations better with every purchase. Three: protocol adoption — once agent platforms integrate our API, switching costs are high. It's the same dynamic as Stripe: easy to start, hard to leave."

**Q: "What are the biggest risks?"**
A: [Be honest. Investors respect this.]
"Three risks. One: agent adoption could be slower than we expect — we mitigate this by having a human-facing product too. Two: a major player could enter aggressively — we mitigate by owning the open protocol, which is more attractive to agents than a walled garden. Three: affiliate margins could compress — we mitigate by building direct merchant relationships and eventually offering promoted listings."

## Tier 2: Harder Questions

**Q: "Your Year 3 projections seem aggressive."**
A: "They are. That's the upside case. Our conservative case is 1M users and $10M revenue by Year 3. Even at conservative, the LTV:CAC ratio makes this a strong business. We're building for the bull case but our economics work in the bear case too."

**Q: "How is this different from a ChatGPT plugin?"**
A: "A plugin is a feature. Fiber is infrastructure. ChatGPT plugins come and go — they're at the mercy of OpenAI's platform decisions. Fiber is a protocol layer that any agent can use. We're not a feature on someone else's platform. We're the platform itself."

**Q: "Why should we invest in seed when the market isn't proven?"**
A: "Because seed is when you get protocol-layer economics. If you wait for the market to be proven, you're paying Series B prices. The question is: do you believe agents are going mainstream? If yes, they'll need commerce infrastructure. If you want to own a piece of that infrastructure at seed valuation, now is the time."
